Garage to home office

What an office conversion needs that a snug doesn't

  • All-day thermal comfort: you'll occupy this room longer than any other in the house. We insulate to current Part L standards on every conversion — but for offices we also model where the desk sits relative to the window and radiator, because a compliant room can still have a cold corner.
  • Power where you'll actually need it: a double socket per wall is a living-room spec. An office gets a dedicated ring with sockets planned around the desk wall, plus provision for monitors, chargers and a heater-rated spur — no extension leads snaking across the floor.
  • Wired data: Wi-Fi through two brick walls is a lottery. We run CAT6 from your router as standard on office jobs, terminated properly. Video calls stop being a gamble.
  • Acoustic separation: an insulated stud lining quietens the room considerably; where the office shares a wall with the living room or a neighbour, we add acoustic insulation in the lining for call privacy in both directions.
  • Daylight for screens: the new window in the old door opening is your main light source — we size and position it to light the room without glaring onto a screen, and specify trickle vents so fresh air doesn't require an open window in February.
The fine print nobody mentions

Working from a conversion — the honest caveats

Business rates: ordinary home working in a converted garage does not trigger business rates. The picture only changes if part of your home is used exclusively and commercially — regular client visits, employees on site, or a room that's never domestic. For a normal remote-work office, there's nothing to do.

Capital gains tax: similar logic. Keep the room dual-use in character (an office that could be a snug) and your home's full private-residence relief is unaffected. A room used exclusively for business can complicate that relief. We're builders, not accountants — but we'll flag it so you can ask yours the right question.

Home insurance: tell your insurer you work from home and that the garage is now habitable space. It's usually a no-cost update, but an unnotified conversion can complicate a claim.

Planning: the same rules as any conversion — usually permitted development, with the conservation-area, listed-building and new-build-estate exceptions covered on our main conversion page. Home working itself needs no permission while the property remains primarily a home.

FAQ

Home office questions

Why not just buy a garden office pod?

Pods have their place, but compare like for like: a pod at a similar price is a smaller structure at the end of a cold walk, on Wi-Fi, often without plumbing-ready services, and it depreciates like a shed. A converted garage is part of your house's insulated envelope, on mains everything, and it appraises as habitable floor area when you sell.

Can I still use it as a spare room?

Yes — and we'd encourage designing it that way. A sofa bed and built-in storage make it an office five days a week and a guest room at Christmas, which is also the configuration that keeps the tax position simple.

What about meetings with clients at home?

Occasional visits are no issue. If your business means regular customer footfall or staff working from the room, the planning and rates picture changes — mention it at the survey and we'll give you a straight answer before you spend anything.
