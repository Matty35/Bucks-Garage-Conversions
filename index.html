Local knowledge

We know Aylesbury's estates — and their small print

A large share of the homes we convert are on newer developments — Kingsbrook, Berryfields, Buckingham Park, Fairford Leys and similar estates. Many of these houses carry restrictive covenants that say the garage must stay a garage, even when planning rules would let you convert it tomorrow.

Most firms never mention this. We check for it at the survey, and if a covenant applies we'll tell you exactly what the route through looks like — developer consent forms, admin fees, or an indemnity policy — before you commit a penny to the build.

We also work to the current Buckinghamshire Council building control regime (not the old district councils), so applications go in right the first time.

The three checks we run before quoting

1. Covenants — does your deed restrict garage use? 2. Planning — is your home in a conservation area, or has permitted development been removed? 3. Party wall — will the design touch a wall you share with next door?

Straight answers

The questions everyone asks first

Do I need planning permission?

Usually not. Around nine in ten garage conversions are internal works covered by permitted development. The exceptions: conservation areas, listed buildings, flats, and — common on newer Aylesbury estates — homes where the developer's planning consent removed permitted development rights or a deed covenant restricts garage use. We check all of this at the survey.

Are building regulations still required if planning isn't?

Yes, always. Turning a garage into a habitable room means meeting the regulations for structure, fire safety, insulation, ventilation, damp-proofing and electrics — and getting a completion certificate at the end. Every project we do is signed off by building control.

My house is a new build with a covenant — is it hopeless?

No. Developer covenants are usually about controlling how the estate looks while houses are still being sold. Most developers will consent to a well-designed conversion, sometimes via an application form and an admin fee. Where consent isn't practical, an indemnity policy can often cover the risk. We'll map the route for your specific deed before any work starts.

Will I need a party wall agreement?

Only if the design physically alters a wall you share with a neighbour — cutting in a steel beam, forming a new opening, or excavating within three metres of their structure. A purely internal conversion that leaves the shared wall alone usually doesn't trigger the Party Wall Act. We flag it at the design stage so there are no surprise surveyor bills.

Will losing the garage hurt my house value?

It depends on your street. Where driveways are generous, a converted garage almost always adds more than it costs. Where parking is tight, losing the only off-street space can cancel the gain. We give you our honest read at the survey — including whether a partial conversion is the smarter move.
