Single vs integral

Which garage have you got?

Most common in Bucks

Integral

Built into the house, under the same roof, usually with a door from the hallway. The simplest conversion — heating and electrics are close by — but fire separation between house and old garage must be brought up to standard.

Shares one wall

Attached

Bolted onto the side of the house. Usually converted with a new opening knocked through, which is where structural steels — and sometimes the Party Wall Act — come in.

Standalone

Detached

A separate building. Convertible, but running heating, water and power across the garden adds cost, and self-contained use can change the planning picture. We'll advise honestly on whether it stacks up.

Building regulations · the actual detail

What building control will check — and how we pass it

Most builders' websites stop at "we comply with building regulations". Here's what compliance physically means in a garage conversion, so you can see the work you're paying for.

  • Floor level & damp (Part C): garage slabs typically sit 50–150mm below the house floor with no membrane beneath. We lay a 1200-gauge damp-proof membrane lapped into the wall DPC, then insulation and screed — bringing the finished floor level with the house for a step-free threshold.
  • Thermal performance (Part L): walls, floor and ceiling insulated to current U-value targets. A single-skin garage wall gets an insulated stud lining; the old door opening becomes a properly insulated cavity infill.
  • Ventilation (Part F): background ventilation (trickle vents) plus purge ventilation via an opening window; mechanical extract where we're adding a WC or utility.
  • Fire safety (Part B): for integral garages, an FD30 fire door and fire-rated separation where the room meets the rest of the house, plus mains-linked smoke detection where the layout requires it.
  • Escape windows (Part B): if the room could be slept in, the window must give a clear openable area of at least 0.33m², minimum 450mm in both height and width, with the opening no more than 1100mm above floor level.
  • Structure (Part A): lintel checks over the old door opening, and engineer-designed steels where we form new openings into the house.
  • Electrics (Part P): all new wiring on certified circuits, with the certificates passed to you at completion.

At the end you receive a completion certificate from building control. Without it, the room legally isn't a habitable space — and it will surface in every future sale. We don't consider a job finished until that certificate exists.

Planning, covenants & party walls

The paperwork trio — checked before we quote

Permitted development vs planning permission

Around 90% of single garage conversions are internal works under permitted development — no planning application needed. You will need permission if your home is in a conservation area or is listed, if it's a flat, or if the original planning consent for your estate stripped permitted development rights. That last one is common on newer Aylesbury developments and catches people out. Where it's ambiguous, we recommend a lawful development certificate: a modest fee for certainty your solicitor will thank you for.

New-build covenants

Separate from planning entirely: your deeds may contain a covenant requiring the garage to stay a garage. Planning permission doesn't override it. On estates like Kingsbrook and Berryfields we check the deed position first, then route through developer consent (often a form and an admin fee) or an indemnity policy. More in our guides.

Party wall

The Party Wall Act applies only when we alter a shared wall — cutting in a beam, forming an opening — or excavate within three metres of a neighbour's structure. A conversion that leaves the shared wall untouched usually doesn't trigger it. When it does apply, we tell you at design stage and the notice cost (typically £700–£1,000 per affected neighbour if a surveyor is appointed) goes in the budget up front, not as a surprise.

FAQ

Single garage questions

How long does a single garage conversion take?

Three to five weeks on site for a dry room, four to six with plumbing. Building control approval before we start typically adds one to two weeks of lead time, which we run in parallel with scheduling.

Will it look like a converted garage from the street?

Not if it's done properly. We tooth-in brickwork to match your house, align the new window with existing openings, and match the roofline details. The giveaway conversions you've seen are the ones where someone bricked up a door without matching anything.

Is my garage too small to be worth converting?

A typical single garage gives about 14–16m² — roughly the size of a decent double bedroom. It's rarely too small; the question is whether the layout connects well to the house, which is exactly what the free survey answers.

What happens to the stuff currently in the garage?

The honest answer: most garages we convert haven't housed a car in years. For genuine storage needs, a partial conversion keeps the front section as secure storage behind a new door — see our double garage page for how the split works.
