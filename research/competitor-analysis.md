# Competitor Analysis — Garage Conversion SERPs (Aylesbury / Buckinghamshire / UK)

**Date:** 8 July 2026
**Queries analysed:** "garage conversion Aylesbury", "garage conversion cost UK", "garage conversion Buckinghamshire", "convert garage to home office"
**Scope:** Top 5 ranking pages per query — page structure, trust signals, before/after presentation, pricing transparency, and coverage of five priority topics.

## Methodology & limitations

Analysis was built from search-engine results and indexed page content for each competitor (domain-scoped queries per site), plus targeted topic probes to establish who — if anyone — ranks for the five priority gap topics. Direct page-by-page crawling was not possible from this environment (outbound fetches to competitor domains are blocked by the session's network egress policy), so on-page details below reflect what each site surfaces in search indexes: headings, indexed sections, sitelinks, and content summaries. Findings marked *(inferred)* are based on indexed content rather than a full-page render and should be spot-checked in a browser before being treated as final. No competitor text has been copied; all notes are structural observations.

## Query 2: "garage conversion cost UK"

SERP make-up: national authority content — trade-body guide (FMB), platform cost guides (Checkatrade, MyJobQuote), a design-and-build firm (Resi), and an aggregator/content site (houseUP). Parking/value — **partial** (notes some buyers pay a premium to keep a garage — one of the only competitors to acknowledge the trade-off). ## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Trust-signal bar is low locally.** Only one local competitor (Garage Conversion Specialists Ltd) stacks guarantee + insurance + accreditation + gallery, and its site is dated. Directories win trust by aggregation. A local firm with named reviews, a written guarantee, insurance details, and building-control sign-off evidence per project would exceed every local competitor.
2. **Before/after is almost universally absent.** Across 20 pages, only ~3 have structured project evidence, and none of the local Bucks/Aylesbury builders present before→after pairs with project parameters (garage type, use, duration, cost band, regs sign-off date). This is the cheapest differentiation available.
3. **Pricing transparency splits the field.** Directories/national guides publish numbers; local builders almost universally don't. The EMD microsite ranks on the Aylesbury query largely because it publishes tiered local prices. Publishing honest local cost bands with named cost drivers is table stakes to compete.
4. **Nobody owns "integral vs attached vs detached" locally**, despite it driving cost, party wall exposure, and fire-safety requirements. Only UK Garage Conversions has an integral-garage page (thin).
5. **Local authority accuracy is a differentiator.** Multiple ranking pages reference the defunct district council. Referencing Buckinghamshire Council building control and the (off-SERP but adjacent) Milton Keynes building-control garage guide correctly signals genuine local expertise.
6. **Home-office query is a green field for UK trade content** — the UK-compliance angle (regs for a habitable workspace, business rates/CGT caveats, data cabling, Part L) has no owner.

## Prioritised content gap list

Ranked by (a) zero-to-weak competitor coverage, (b) proven search/forum demand, (c) local relevance to Aylesbury/Bucks, (d) conversion intent.

### P1 — New-build estate covenants (highest-value gap, zero SERP coverage)
No ranking page on any of the four queries covers restrictive covenants — yet Aylesbury's housing stock is disproportionately new-build estate (Kingsbrook, Berryfields, Buckingham Park, Fairford Leys, Watermead), where garage-use covenants are common and forum demand is visible. Build a dedicated guide: how to check deeds, developer consent process (application forms, admin fees), deed of release vs indemnity policy vs Upper Tribunal route, and how covenants interact with PD rights (consent needed even when planning isn't). Reference the major estate developers' processes generically. This is simultaneously unique, local, and a pre-sales objection handler.

### P2 — Building regs in practical detail (escape windows, floor level, insulation, ventilation)
Top-5 coverage never goes past naming the Parts. Publish a compliance page with the details searchers currently only find in council PDFs: escape-window minimums (0.33m² clear opening, 450mm min dimensions, ≤1,100mm cill) for sleeping rooms, garage floor step-down and DPM/insulation/screed build-up to match house floor level, wall tanking, Part F ventilation options, Part L U-values, FD30 doors for integral garages, and building notice vs full plans routes with Buckinghamshire Council fees. Winning the featured snippet for escape-window/floor-level questions is realistic because no top-5 page answers them.

### P3 — Party wall for attached garages (when the Act does and doesn't apply)
FMB and MyJobQuote price it (~£700–£1,000/neighbour) but nobody explains the trigger logic. Publish a decision-style explainer: internal-only conversions usually outside the Act; cutting in beams/lintels/openings into a shared wall = Section 2 notice; excavation within 3m = Section 6; informal neighbour chats have no legal force. High relevance to semi-detached/link-detached and shared-garage-block stock in Aylesbury. Position as a cost-and-delay de-risking service ("we tell you at survey whether the Act applies").

### P4 — Parking loss and property value (the honest trade-off page)
Local competitors only claim uplift (10–20%); none address the counterweight buyers and surveyors actually weigh: losing off-street parking, buyer-pool narrowing where parking is scarce, and when a partial conversion is the better call. An honest calculator-style page (uplift range vs parking penalty by street type, driveway retention, EV-charging consideration) would be unique on all four SERPs and builds trust precisely because it argues against overselling.

### P5 — Permitted development vs planning permission, localised to Buckinghamshire Council
National coverage is adequate but generic; local coverage is one-liners, and several competitors cite the abolished district council. Publish a local decision guide: PD basics, the ~10%-need-permission reality, exception cases (conservation areas — e.g. Aylesbury old town, listed buildings, flats, already-used PD rights, new-build PD removal conditions — cross-link to P1), lawful development certificate rationale and Buckinghamshire Council fees/process. Accuracy about the unitary authority is itself a differentiator.

### P6 — Garage-to-home-office, UK compliance edition
The query's SERP is US-heavy and design-led; no UK builder covers the compliance layer: regs for a habitable workroom, heating/insulation for all-day occupancy, data/power provision, plus the caveats nobody mentions (business rates and CGT only in specific circumstances, home-insurance notification). A UK-specific, regs-aware home-office page has a clear ranking path and matches post-2020 hybrid-work demand.

### P7 — Local proof assets (before/after case studies with parameters)
Not a topic gap but a format gap: no Aylesbury/Bucks competitor publishes structured before→after case studies (estate name, garage type, use, duration, cost band, building-control completion certificate). Two or three of these, tagged to named estates (e.g. a Berryfields or Kingsbrook project), would outperform every gallery currently ranking and feed P1's estate-specific angle.

### P8 — Published local price bands
Table stakes rather than a gap: the only local page publishing tiered Aylesbury pricing is a thin EMD microsite. Publish honest bands (single/double, dry/wet room, integral/attached/detached) with named cost drivers and what's included/excluded (drawings, building control, party wall, LDC) mirroring FMB's checklist structure — then localise with Buckinghamshire labour-rate context.
