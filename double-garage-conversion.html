Full vs partial

Two ways to convert a double

Option A — partial

Half room, half garage

  • Rear section becomes a warm room of ~14–18m²
  • Front keeps its garage door — storage, bikes, workshop
  • Keeps covenant and parking questions simpler on many estates
  • Typically £14,500 – £19,500
Option B — full

The whole footprint

  • One large space of ~28–36m², or split into two rooms
  • Both door openings infilled with matched brickwork and windows
  • Scope for utility + snug, office + gym, or a family room
  • Typically £19,500 – £32,000

The partial route matters more than most firms admit: where a covenant requires garage parking, or where losing all off-street storage would dent resale, converting the rear half often captures most of the benefit with none of the downside.

Structure

What's different about a double

  • Two openings, or one wide one: twin doors are infilled like two singles; a single wide door means checking the existing lintel or beam is fit to carry the new infill and window loads.
  • Internal dividing walls: splitting the space into two rooms adds stud partitions with acoustic insulation — worth it when one side is a home office or bedroom.
  • Heating capacity: 30m² of new heated space is a genuine load. We check your boiler's capacity at the survey rather than discovering the shortfall in December; electric underfloor heating is often the better answer for one of the two rooms.
  • Drainage runs: a double often has room for a utility or WC at the house end where drainage connections are shortest — we plan wet areas around your existing runs to keep cost down.
FAQ

Double garage questions

How long does a double take?

Five to eight weeks for a full conversion depending on wet rooms and structural work; a partial conversion usually runs three to five weeks, similar to a single.

Can a partial conversion be extended later?

Yes — we design partials so the dividing wall can come out and the front section can be converted as a second phase, reusing the same floor build-up approach. Doing it in two stages costs a little more overall but spreads the spend.

Does a detached double work as an annexe?

Structurally, often yes — but a self-contained annexe changes the planning position and may need a full application rather than permitted development. See our bedroom & annexe page for the honest version of that conversation.
